# Can ChartStep make money?

**Yes, it is a plausible small software business. Revenue is unproven.** A working simulator and a better design are useful foundations; neither establishes demand, repeat use or willingness to pay.

The strongest initial proposition is a guided practice space for a beginner cohort: teach one concept, let the student make a decision, explain the result, and help an instructor discuss their reasoning. That is a clearer starting position than competing on the number of chart tools.

## What the existing market tells us

Checked 12 November 2026, using the providers' own pages:

- [FX Replay pricing](https://fxreplay.com/pricing) lists a free tier, Intermediate at $17.99 monthly or $180 annually, and Pro at $35 monthly or $350 annually. Its plans combine replay/backtesting, analytics and journaling.
- [TradeZella's pricing explanation](https://help.tradezella.com/en/articles/8911582-our-pricing) lists Essential at $35 monthly, Pro at $59 and Ultra at $99; it also offers annual billing. Its product includes journaling, replay and backtesting.

These are evidence of paid offers in the category, **not evidence that ChartStep has paying demand**. Comparing these feature-rich products with this MVP does not establish a fair price. My inference: beginner instruction and educator distribution are more credible differentiators than a lower price alone.

## A concrete first pilot

Propose a four-week pilot to one independent educator, with 15–25 students and eight selected exercises. Agree the teaching objective and a small paid pilot fee before adding a large academy system. A suggested test fee is £99 for the cohort, including setup and one feedback session. It is an experiment, not a validated price or an offer currently sold by the website.

Use the current export and a manual weekly instructor discussion initially. Do not claim that the current demo provides shared classes or instructor dashboards. Students currently save progress on their own device. Obtain feedback directly and agree what they choose to share; there is no background analytics collection in the MVP.

Suggested decision thresholds, chosen for this pilot rather than borrowed industry benchmarks:

1. At least 15 students start an exercise.
2. At least 10 complete four exercises and six return in week four.
3. Compare a short new-chart explanation before and after the pilot. Score whether students identify the visible evidence, define risk, and acknowledge uncertainty. Repeating a memorised scenario is insufficient.
4. The educator pays for a second cohort, or two other educators commit to a paid trial.

Record reasons for non-use and drop-off. A small pilot cannot establish broad educational effectiveness, but it can reveal whether the workflow is useful enough to continue.

## Revenue arithmetic, not a forecast

| Hypothetical subscribers | Displayed provisional price | Gross monthly revenue |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 100 students | £9.99 | £999 |
| 10 academies | £79 | £790 |
| 50 academies | £79 | £3,950 |

This is simple multiplication before fees, tax, hosting, support, content production, acquisition and cancellations. It assumes every subscriber pays for that month. It is not profit or a forecast. The £79 tier also needs a clear seat allowance and support scope before it becomes a sustainable offer; “unlimited students and support” would be an untested promise.

## What to build after evidence, not before

If the pilot works, prioritise secure accounts and recovery, academy membership, assignments and a simple instructor progress view. Then implement billing and cancellation. Keep the reliable beginner curriculum, fresh continuous simulation, accessible drawing tools and clear feedback as the core experience.

The present release is an educational, browser-local demo: no account service, payments, brokerage connection, real quotes, shared classroom or measured retention. The website labels paid and academy features as planned. Do not market profitability, guaranteed learning outcomes or simulated gains as investment results.

**Recommendation:** aim for the first paying educator and a renewed cohort before targeting large subscription numbers. Good design earns a trial; useful teaching and repeat use earn the renewal.
